# Institut national des appellations d'origine
- formed in **1935**
- establishes and regulates geographical indications ([[Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée]])
	- proposed more stringent rules for grape growing and winemaking in [[Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée|AOC]]s
- now called the **Institut national de l’origine et de la qualité**
- also manages foods such as cheese
## Key Regulations
1. All grapes must **originate** from designated region
	- [[Negociants]] (some winemakers) can buy grapes/wine and finish and market
2. Only certain **grape varieties** permitted to be grown
	- **terroir** and variety **matched** for highest **quality**
	- may change with climate change
3. Viticulture
	- vines must reach **correct maturity** (>2 years)
	- grapes must reach **correct ripeness** (minimum sugars)
		- possible restrictions on acidity
	- restrictions on **pruning, irrigation**
		- irrigation only allowed during vine establishment & drought
4. **Maximum yield** levels dependent on size of land
	- expressed in hectolitres (hundred litres) per hectare
5. Wines must be **typical & consistent** for that region
	- sensory review
	- e.g. can't make a white merlot in [[Bordeaux]]
6. Specifies some rules for **vinification**
	- e.g. [[Pre-fermentation Adjustments#Chaptalization|Chaptalization]], acidification, ABV levels, [[Aging]], use of oak
