# Wine and Health
- historically believed to be good for your health
## Historical Perspectives
### Possible Beneficial Compounds
- [[Phenolic Compounds]] may be beneficial
	- tannins, flavonoids, pigments
	- Quercetin
		- antioxidant
		- less low density lipoprotein (LDL) oxidation/plaque formation
			- may help prevent blood clots
		- reduces inflammation
		- anti-cancer agent
		- may contribute to wine headaches?
	- Resveratrol
		- reduces inflammation
		- lowers LDL
		- may also help prevent blood clots
		- promotes increase in good cholesterol (high density lipoprotein - HDL)
	- Epicatechins
		- anti-cancer agents
		- may also help prevent blood clots
### California "J" Curve
- correlational study
- study published in **1981** by A. L. Klatsky
- suggests that a moderate intake of wine **every day** reduces risk of premature death
- zero and excessive intakes were associated with a higher risk

### French Paradox
- 60 minutes CBS program aired in 1991
- purports that French eat richer (higher saturated fat) foods, don't exercise but have 10 times fewer heart failures than Americans
	- at the time, Americans ate leaner food and exercised more
- French drink 10 times more wine than Americans
	- inverse correlation?
- sales of red wines in the US went up 44% in 2 weeks
- later scrutiny indicates that French diets fall under category of [[Mediterranean Diet]]
## Newer Epidemiological Studies
- blood pressure benefits?
	- some research indicates long-term alcohol consumption promotes high blood pressure
	- increasing number of drinks/day correlated with increased blood pressure
- French Paradox?
	- negative effects of wine may be counteracted by [[Mediterranean Diet]]
- Anti-cancer?
	- alcohol intake linked to increased risk of cancer (oral, pharynx, larynx, colorectal, breast, oesophagus and liver)
	- studied are less focused on wine and more on alcohol in general
## Problems with drinking-health studies
### American Journal of Preventive Medicine (Naimi et al. 2005)
- Survey of 250,000 Americans
- **abstainers had more risk factors** compared to the drinking crowd
	- e.g. obesity to high blood pressure and diabetes
	- pre-existing conditions that cause them to obstain from alcohol
- Likely skewed results from J-curve research
	- i.e. the 0 drink people were unhealthier to begin with
### Addiction Research & Theory (Fillmore et al. 2007)
- Meta-analysis of 54 studies 
- 1974-2004: no cardiac protection from alcohol
- Control groups included people who had reduced or stopped drinking for health reasons
